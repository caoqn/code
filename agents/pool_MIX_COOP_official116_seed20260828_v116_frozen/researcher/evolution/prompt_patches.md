- When solving assignment or matching puzzles from structured documents, first extract tables with headers intact, then validate the meaning and direction of each column by matching known attributes before inferring unmatched entities or failures.
- When computing counts from external profile APIs, first infer the benchmark's intended counting unit and snapshot date; reconcile deduplicated groups, raw entries, work types, and temporal cutoff against the question before averaging.
- When querying issue trackers for a label that may have been renamed, search current and historical label names, enumerate paginated results, intersect all requested labels/components locally, then verify the earliest candidate via its timeline events and canonical record.
- When identifying dated promotions from summary tables, treat displayed feature/main-page dates as potentially different from the actual promotion date; verify the exact status-change timestamp in the underlying archived discussion or official promotion log.
- When direct repository access is unavailable, avoid speculative fixes; obtain concrete source snippets, failing assertions, or test fixtures from teammates before advising on field mappings, and clearly label any remaining hypotheses.
- When a question specifies a data source and a qualifier such as “unadjusted” or “split-adjusted,” verify that source’s own display/API semantics and definition before substituting external historical evidence; explicitly test candidate years against the source series and clarify whether corporate actions are already back-adjusted.
- When a question asks for historical counts from a dynamic website, first capture the exact snapshot/filter parameters and preserve the returned facet counts; do not substitute current live totals, and clearly distinguish directly observed historical data from drift-prone approximations.
- When a question asks for the material/compound studied in an article, identify the study's primary material named in the title, abstract, or main objective before considering secondary probes, coatings, substrates, or measurement additives; explicitly map each candidate to the question's wording and report the one matching the requested role.
- When researching historical changelogs with an ambiguous date, first identify the relevant release/version from the official release history, then inspect the canonical raw changelog and quote the exact section heading and entry; clearly distinguish release date from development or publication date.
- When a primary document is a PDF that cannot be locally parsed, seek an authoritative OCR or full-text derivative from the same publisher/archive, verify its metadata and effective date, and quote the exact relevant sequence rather than relying on search snippets.
- For open-ended external research, time-box exploration: dispatch a focused query immediately, set a small attempt budget, and stop once one authoritative source plus a corroborating clue supports the answer; avoid repeated searches or back-and-forth when evidence is unlikely to improve.
